# Illustration briefs — for a visual model

Eight scenes the archive can defend from documents. Each has a **prompt**, a
**negative** line, and a **what must be right** note so a wrong detail can be
caught. House rules for all of them:

- Aspect **3:2 landscape** unless stated. **No text, no lettering, no captions,
  no title strips, no watermarks, no signatures anywhere in the frame** — the
  site adds its own. Put this in the negative prompt every time; two of the
  first three came back with a caption burned into the picture, and one of them
  invented an inscription on a real carved stone.
- No modern objects, no anachronistic clothing, no invented heraldry.
- **No faces of named individuals.** No portrait of any of these people
  survives, so figures are distant, turned away, or in silhouette. A face
  invented and then captioned with a real name is a forgery; keep them small.
- Muted, aged palette: bone, umber, iron grey, oxblood, olive. Nothing candy.
- Where a style is given, hold to it — mixing "oil painting" and "engraving"
  gives the flat vector look we are trying to avoid.

---

## 1. Imotski, the evening of 25 July 1717

> An eighteenth-century topographical engraving, etched copperplate, fine
> cross-hatching in a single sepia-black ink on laid paper with visible plate
> mark and foxing. A steep karst crag of bare pale limestone rising above a
> vast circular sinkhole whose sheer walls drop to a small dark lake far below.
> On the crest of the crag a small Ottoman-era stone fortress with square keep,
> crenellated curtain wall and one round tower; a triangular war-banner flies
> from the wall. Below and to the right, a low huddle of stone houses with
> shallow tiled roofs and one squat campanile, the town of a mountain frontier.
> On the plain to the left, a distant column of ox-drawn field artillery on a
> rough track, tiny in scale. Bare stony ground, a few thorn bushes, no forest.
> Far horizon: a long barren limestone ridge. Late afternoon light raking from
> the left, long shadows, high thin cloud rendered in horizontal ruled lines.
> Cartographer's viewpoint — slightly elevated, three-quarters, wide.

**Negative:** colour, painterly brushwork, modern buildings, lush greenery,
tourists, flags with recognisable modern emblems, people in the foreground,
text, borders, frames.

**What must be right:** the fortress sits *on* the crag, not floating; the
sinkhole is a funnel with visibly receding walls, not a drum or a disc; the
artillery is far off and small — the whole point is that it arrived too late.

## 2. The house at Gologorica, 1711

> A quiet architectural study in muted oil, in the manner of a nineteenth-century
> topographical watercolour. A modest two-storey stone house on a bare Istrian
> hill village lane, built of rough limestone blocks with dressed quoins, a
> shallow tiled roof, small deep-set windows with stone surrounds, and a low
> arcaded portico of three round arches along the ground floor. Above the door
> a rectangular carved stone lintel bearing an incised inscription and, beside
> it, a small worn coat of arms. A stone bench by the door. Dry-stone walls, a
> fig tree, hens. Overcast northern-Adriatic light, no shadows, a village of
> ten houses and a bell tower behind. Restrained, documentary, unromantic.

**Negative:** grandeur, palace scale, Tuscan cypresses, warm Mediterranean
sunshine, flowers in window boxes, **any lettering anywhere in the image**,
inscribed text, carved words, letters on the tablet, a caption or title strip,
a signature, figures in relief panels.

**Say this explicitly in the prompt:** *the carved stone tablet above the door
is blank and weathered — worn smooth, no letters, no inscription, no text of any
kind.* The first attempt wrote invented pseudo-Latin across it, and this is the
one stone in the archive whose real text is settled; a picture of it carrying
made-up words is the thing most likely to be mistaken for the real inscription.

**What must be right:** it is a *palazzetto* — the biggest house in a poor
village, not a mansion. Arcaded portico and a carved arms panel on the façade;
those two things are what the architectural historian records.

## 3. The road of the Furlans, 1679

> Muted oil, low horizon, cold light. A file of six or seven men walking a
> stony track downhill out of alpine country toward a dry limestone plateau.
> They carry loom parts, bolts of coarse cloth and shears on their backs and on
> one small pack mule. Late-seventeenth-century peasant-artisan dress: coarse
> undyed wool, felt hats, leg wrappings, wooden-soled shoes. Behind and above
> them, steep green Carnian valleys with a stone campanile far back; ahead and
> below, the barren white karst opening out. Seen from behind and to the side —
> no faces. Autumn, thin high cloud, the light going.

**Negative:** heroic posture, uniforms, weapons, banners, faces, a wagon,
summer greenery in the foreground, romantic golden light.

**What must be right:** these are *weavers*, not soldiers or refugees. The
burden is a trade, carried. They are leaving mountains and entering karst — the
two landscapes must both be visible in one frame.

## 4. The mills at Perinuša

> Muted oil, level viewpoint, damp air. A long low range of stone watermills
> strung along a clear fast river in a bare karst plain, each with a small
> arched tailrace and a paddle wheel; a multi-arched stone causeway on squat
> piers crossing the river beside them, its cutwaters each set at a slightly
> different angle. Behind the mills a plain stone manor house with a walled
> garden and a high blank wall, and a stone stair rising to a first-floor door
> wide enough for a horse. Tall poplars along both banks. Sacks of grain
> stacked under the low stone-slab roofs; two mules tethered; men waiting,
> distant, indistinct. Early morning mist. A hot bare mountain wall far behind.

**Negative:** picturesque ruin, ivy, tourists, mill wheels turning
dramatically, waterfalls, autumn colour, faces.

**What must be right:** *many* mills in a row, not one; the causeway on piers;
poplars; and the exterior stone stair to the first floor — horses were kept
upstairs out of the river damp.

## 5. The Salamon palace, Gračišće

> Precise architectural elevation in the manner of a conservation survey
> drawing, ink and pale wash on paper. A two-storey palace of finely dressed
> pale limestone standing immediately beside a fortified town gate. First
> floor: a Venetian floral-Gothic double-lancet window with a slender central
> colonette and a quatrefoil above, opening onto a small stone balcony on
> carved corbels with a turned balustrade. Second floor: three simple windows
> with semicircular arches under a running band of classical dentil ornament.
> Ground floor: a plain arched doorway and two barred openings. Shallow tiled
> roof. Flat frontal view, no perspective distortion, neutral even light,
> plain background.

**Negative:** perspective, sky, people, colour beyond a pale wash, ruin,
scaffolding, vegetation.

**What must be right:** Gothic on the first floor, Renaissance on the second —
the two styles alternate up the façade. That contrast is the whole reason the
building is famous.

## 6. Antonio the stonecutter, Gračišće, about 1870

> Muted oil, workshop interior opening to a yard. A man in his forties in
> shirtsleeves and a leather apron cutting a block of white Istrian limestone
> with a mallet and a toothed chisel; stone dust in the light from the door.
> Around him: a half-finished window surround, a trough, a squared kerbstone, a
> broken tomb slab. Simple tools on the wall — points, claws, a wooden square,
> a straight-edge. Through the doorway, a bare hill village lane and a bell
> tower. He is seen in three-quarter profile, head down at the work, face in
> shadow and not detailed. Nothing sentimental; a man at his trade at the end
> of the day.

**Negative:** a defined recognisable face, a studio portrait pose, a sculptor's
studio, marble statuary, romantic lighting, modern tools.

**What must be right:** *lapidarius* — building stone and grave slabs, not art.
Istrian limestone is white to pale grey. The workshop is poor.

## 7. Crikvenica, the winter of 1943

> Muted oil, dusk, cold. A narrow stone lane in a small Adriatic coastal town
> in winter, wet cobbles, shuttered windows, one bare lamp. Two or three armed
> men in mixed civilian and partisan dress walking a middle-aged man away up
> the lane; all figures small, seen from a distance and from behind, faces not
> visible. A woman standing in a doorway, back to the viewer. Sea and grey sky
> at the end of the lane. Restrained, documentary, no melodrama, no blood, no
> violence depicted — the moment before anything happens.

**Negative:** violence, weapons pointed, uniforms with identifiable insignia,
swastikas, red stars, faces, crowds, dramatic lighting, gore.

**What must be right:** understatement. This is a family's worst day rendered
as an ordinary street at dusk. Nothing in it should be exciting.

---

## 8. Pfullingen, 1944

> Muted oil, flat grey light. A Swabian textile mill town in a narrow valley:
> long brick weaving sheds with sawtooth north-light roofs, a tall chimney,
> half-timbered houses on the slope behind, wooded hills above. In the
> foreground a line of foreign workers walking to the shift on a cinder path
> beside a canalised stream — plain worn clothes, small figures, seen from
> behind. Early morning, frost, breath visible. Nothing that identifies a
> regime; just a mill town working in winter.

**Negative:** uniforms, guards, wire, insignia, flags, faces, a camp, snow-globe
prettiness, warm light.

**What must be right:** this is a *works*, not a camp — the men were labour in
a factory town, billeted and marched to work. Sawtooth roofs and the valley
are the identifying features.

## Where to put the files

    zuane-imotski-1717.webp

    road-of-the-furlans-1679.webp
    perinusa-mills.webp
    salamon-palace.webp
    antonio-lapidarius.webp
    crikvenica-1943.webp

plus a `-t.webp` thumbnail of each at about 640px wide. The site will use the
file where one exists and fall back to the drawn plate where none does, and
every one of them will be captioned as a constructed illustration with the
model named — the same rule the archive applies to everything else.
