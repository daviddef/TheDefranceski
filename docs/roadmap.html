Question one

Am I finished with FamilySearch? No.

Three separate things have been done there, and three have not.

Done

  • Surname searches across the indexed record set, in every spelling variant.
  • A sixty-parish sweep by place across Istria and the Kvarner — the new "Every Town" page.
  • Waypoint lookups for register holdings, parish by parish.

Not done

  • The browse-only films. The big one. Gračišće, Gologorica, Crikvenica and Klenovica are all on FamilySearch as unindexed images. No search will ever find them. They must be read page by page.
  • The Family Tree side. Only the historical records have been worked. The collaborative tree, its sources, and its attached memories are untouched.
  • Italy, Austria and the Americas by place. The sweep covered Istria. Friuli, Trieste's hinterland, Brazil and Argentina have only been searched by name.

The single most valuable unopened thing in the project

The family already holds 1,467 page images of the Pazin registers for Gologorica (folders ZM34K-214 and ZM34K-215) and 265 images of Senj births 1878–1894. Nobody has read them straight through. Everything the archive knows about Gologorica came from two certified extracts and a Status Animarum print — the rest of those 1,467 pages has never been looked at. A systematic read is the highest-yield work available and needs no archive, no fee and no permission.

Question two

The research ladder

Ordered by yield per hour, not by interest. The top three would each change a page.

Send this week

Arolsen tracing case 349.822

Eight documents, 1954–1984, withheld online. Somebody was asking the ITS about Petar for thirty years and we do not know who. The letter is written and sitting in requests/arolsen-td-349822.md. It needs your postal address and a send.

Write this week

The Geneanet member who signs himself "carnia"

He has published the only rounded De Franceschi household at Mione — five generations of the dal Ginut house with trades, house-names and Friulian notes — and he has clearly worked the Ovaro registers directly. Ask him two questions: does GioBatta's line go back past Paolo, and did any De Franceschi of Mione leave for Istria? He is the best-placed human being we have identified.

effort: 30 minneeds: Geneanet account
New — from the sweep

Svetvinčenat, 1650–1720

Eleven indexed entries between 1674 and 1710 — Mattio, Domenico Gasparo, Zuana, Marina, Maria Maddalena De Franceschi. Older than the Mione headmen's list of 1679, older than the c.1700 arrival Carlo claims, and nobody in this project had ever looked at Svetvinčenat. If those people have parents in the parish book, the tidy story of one Carnian arrival breaks open.

effort: a daywhere: DAPA Pazin, or FamilySearch films

Labin (Albona), 1600–1650

Aloÿsius Defranceschi, 1615. The earliest indexed occurrence of the surname anywhere in Istria — sixty-four years before Mione.

effort: half a day

The 1711 house at Gologorica

Carlo says in the Kobler letters: "Il mio trisavolo fabbricò la nostra casa nel 1711 come da relativa iscrizione." A dated inscription on a standing building. Nobody has established whether the house survives. The Pazin conservation office (Konzervatorski odjel u Puli / Pazin) would know, and Gologorica is small enough to walk in an afternoon.

effort: an email, or a trippayoff: a photograph of the family's own datestone

The bells of Gologorica

Also from the letters: a bell of 1364 reading Magister Nicholaus me fecit, and one of 1573 with four Glagolitic letters Carlo could not read. Are they still in the tower? The Croatian Conservation Institute has a campanological survey; the diocese of Poreč-Pula holds parish inventories.

effort: two emails

Ovaro parish registers before 1806, and the Agrons castle documents

The pre-Napoleonic Ovaro books are in no portal and must be requested from the parish; the Archivio Storico Diocesano at Udine may hold duplicates. Separately, the family's own map records that the De Franceschi held the castle of Agrons on the Degano in the Gorto valley, with documents of 1467 and 1471. Those documents have never been chased. The Micoli Toscano family archive at Mione holds Gorto contracts, including ones naming the Erman who travelled to Istria with Zuane in 1679.

effort: weeks of correspondencepayoff: the deepest possible

Senj death register, 1907–1920

Milka Papić married Zvonimir Vukelić in 1907 and was a widow by December 1920. What killed him is not in the file, and the war and the 1918 influenza both sit in the gap.

effort: half a day

Crikvenica marriages, 1900s–1910s

For Anton Rudolf Defranceski and Ursula Kovačina. It would prove the link to the Kovačina household at house 119, which the archive currently states as inference.

Karlobag registers, to put citations under the ancestor chart

The Karlobag line reaches people born around 1690 and is the deepest thing in the archive — but it rests on an uncited MyHeritage chart. The family already holds scans of Karlobag births 1691–1770 and 1804–1861 and the Stanje duša. This is verification work, not discovery, and it is entirely doable at the desk.

Deeper still — archives nobody has written to

  • Archivio di Stato di Trieste — Austrian Littoral administration, notarial acts, the Luogotenenza files that would carry the nobility question Carlo raises.
  • Archivio Storico Diocesano, Udine — duplicate Ovaro registers before 1806.
  • Državni arhiv u Rijeci — Rijeka is the heaviest cluster in the sweep (66 entries from 1742) and has never been worked directly.
  • Biblioteca Civica "Attilio Hortis", Trieste — Carlo's and Camillo's papers and the Istrian periodicals.
  • Centro di Ricerche Storiche, Rovigno — the standing scholarly body for Istrian history; they answer letters.
  • Archivio di Stato di Venezia — for the Seget purchase, the Valier sale of 1623, and the Candia refugee lists after 1669.
  • Museo Etnografico, Ovaro / Micoli Toscano archive, Mione — the Gorto contracts.
  • Horsham and Ipswich local newspapers — obituaries for Zdravko 1979, Ruža 1996, Anton 1999.
  • NAA (National Archives of Australia) — the 1949 assisted-migration file for Zdravko, Ruža and Anton. They sailed 8 March 1949; that file exists and is releasable.
Question four

What the site itself needs

Sixty-seven pages is a lot of archive and not much story. It reads like a set of findings rather than a thing you can be walked through. This is the work.

01

Make the numbers go somewhere

Every metric tile on the home page is currently a dead end — 1679, zavičaj, 99 burials, 628 people worldwide. Each should be a link to the page that earns it, with a hover state that says where it goes. This is an afternoon's work and it is the single change you asked for most directly.

02

One spine, told once, properly

The story is currently spread over Direct Line, Gologorica, Carnia, Crikvenica, Pfullingen, Senj and Imotski, and a first-time reader has no idea where to start. Build a single narrative walk — eight screens, 1679 to Brisbane — that carries the reader from the Mione list to your own birth in Johannesburg, with every claim linking down into the detail page that proves it. Everything else becomes reference behind it.

03

A real map, on the site, not in Google

Your My Maps has 1,355 markers across ten layers and none of it is on the site. An interactive map — Carnia, Istria, the Kvarner, Germany, Australia, South Africa — with the documented line drawn as one path and the other families as separate colours, would do more for a visitor in ten seconds than any page currently does.

04

A living family tree, not tables

An expandable, pannable descent chart from Giambattista del fu Leonardo down, with confidence colouring (documented / inferred / lore) baked into the node, click-through to the person, and living people simply absent. The data already exists in directline.json and people.json.

05

Put the documents next to the claims

77 plates exist but most sit in a gallery. Every assertion that rests on an image should show a thumbnail of that image inline, clickable to the full plate with its transcription beside it. The Lightbox already does this; it just is not wired everywhere.

06

A timeline that is actually a timeline

Currently a table. It should be a horizontal, zoomable band from 1364 to 2026, with the family's events above the line and the world's below — Venice, the plagues, 1797, 1918, 1943, 1949, 1991 — so that a reader can see that Petar's decision to stay in Germany happens in the same year his brother sails for Australia.

07

Cut and merge

Sixty-seven pages is too many. Namesakes, Your Name, The Name and Diaspora overlap heavily; Explorer, Search and Households are three doors into the same data. Target: around forty pages, with the reference material consolidated behind three or four strong hubs.

08

Say who it is for, on the first screen

Right now the home page opens on findings. It should open on a person — you, in Brisbane, born in Johannesburg, descended from a stonemason on an Istrian hill — and then offer three doors: read the story, look up a person, see the documents. Everything else follows from that.

Standing

Open questions the archive has not closed

Does the Istrian line actually come from Carnia?

Carlo says so privately and never in print. The 1679 list puts a Zuane De Franceschi of Mione in the County of Pazin. No document joins the two. Labin 1615 and Svetvinčenat 1674 now sit awkwardly against the c.1700 arrival date.

Which coast does Poldo belong to?

Born 1901 at Crikvenica, died 1971, photographed with an accordion — but the family tree files him among the nine Imotski children. The one individual genuinely sitting across the seam.

Who was Felix?

Born 14 January 1908 at Crikvenica, standing on the Pfullingen list beside the brothers, in no register and no family file. A late child of Josip, or an early one of Anton Rudolf.

Latin cross or Greek cross?

Carlo's own letter of 1876 says croce greca. The published Blasonario blazon, credited to "C. de F." — Carlo himself — says croce latina. The site currently draws the Latin. One of the two is a transcription error and it is probably not the private letter.
